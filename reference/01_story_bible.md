## 1. THE PROMISE OF THE BOOK

A dying Hill Country widow the whole town calls "Miss Ruth" rides a losing high school football team's bus through one last season, and only after her murder at Christmas 2011 does anyone learn that she was a teenage nurse's aide at Pearl Harbor, an American spy in occupied France, a survivor of Ravensbrück, and the secret wife of a Black fighter pilot she married in a French village church in 1944, a marriage that was a crime in her own state. She carried all of it in silence for sixty-six years. **[JOHN + panel decision]**

The story is told by an old Black man, Sam's nephew, by a stone fireplace on Christmas Eve 2026, after his grandchildren find her World War II trunk in the attic by the light of a full moon. **[JOHN]**

Every place, date and public figure checks out. Readers go down the rabbit hole and end asking one question: **was she real?** **[JOHN]**

### Tone rules (John's standing rules for this book)

- Real faith, real people, harsh outlines of people struggling with real things. No goofy Christian-movie writing. Nobody is converted by a speech. God is silent at times, and the story lets that silence stand. **[JOHN]**
- The horrors of evil and war are shown as they were, on the body, without a narrator softening them. Evil wears uniforms, pastor's collars under swastika banners, lawyer's suits and white coats. **[JOHN]**
- Forgiveness only where there is repentance (Luke 17:3–4; Matthew 18:22). The repentant guard is forgiven; the unrepentant murderers get justice. **[JOHN]**
- Vivid pictures over abstraction. Every character carries a hidden pain behind the visible problem. **[JOHN]**
- Ruth's faith is shown inside the horror, never standing apart from it.
- **Sam is a full man on the page**: his own scenes, his own voice in his letters, his own reason to fly. He is never only Ruth's love interest. **[critic's condition]**

### Narrative models **[JOHN]**

- *The Princess Bride* — the old man telling, the bored skeptical kid interrupting and getting hooked.
- *Titanic* — one object starts the telling; the teller holds one secret back until the end.
- *Benjamin Button* — a diary read aloud while a storm closes in.
- *Remember the Titans* — opens and closes at a funeral; a football season told as history; race.
- *Forrest Gump* — the character brushes real history and real people at every turn.
- *The Notebook* — a love that starts in childhood, is torn apart by the world, and runs the length of a life.
